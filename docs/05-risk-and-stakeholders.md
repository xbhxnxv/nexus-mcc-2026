# Risk and stakeholders

## Key risks

| Risk | Level | Mitigation |
|------|-------|------------|
| BECCS pilot cost overrun | High | First-of-kind interface with a live biomass plant. NEC4 Option C target cost with gain/pain share, plus an £80–120k pre-feasibility study before RIBA Stage 2 |
| MEP / skilled trades shortage | High | Sector-wide shortage documented by CITB, and services are around 40% of construction cost. Early contractor engagement via the Constructing West Midlands framework; sub-contractor rates locked at RIBA Stage 3 |
| Brownfield site conditions | Medium | Contamination and unknown ground risk. Phase 1/2 ground investigation at Stage 1, with contingency earmarked for remediation |
| Technology obsolescence | Medium | Computing hardware dates quickly. Equipment procurement phased to the Year 3 fit-out; modular lab design; specifications not locked at Stage 2 |
| Funding gap (Tier 1/2) | Medium | £3.2m working capital reserve; secure UKRI and industry MOUs before the Phase 2 contractor appointment |

The financial model adds a sixth risk that the risk register does not name: the operating gap. Under the revised £4.5m OPEX and the auditable base revenue of £2.865m, the reserve does not last the planned five years. That is covered in [03 — financial model](03-financial-model.md) and is arguably the most material risk to the facility's steady-state viability.

## Stakeholders

Stakeholders are mapped on Mendelow's power–interest matrix.

| Quadrant | Stakeholders | Engagement |
|----------|--------------|------------|
| Manage closely (high power, high interest) | University leadership, Birmingham City Council, WMCA, Tyseley Energy Park partners | Steering committee, executive gateway reviews, pre-application planning, technical coordination |
| Keep satisfied (high power, low interest) | Funding bodies (UKRI, Innovate UK), emergency services, transport authorities | Gateway reviews, formal reporting, HAZID workshops, safety planning |
| Keep informed (low power, high interest) | Students, local residents, local businesses, environmental groups | Student forums, public drop-in sessions, business roundtables, sustainability workshops |
| Monitor (low power, low interest) | General public, wider academic community | Website updates, annual public impact report |

## Sustainability

Sustainability is framed against the APM four pillars:

- **Environmental** — BREEAM Excellent target; on-site BESS and biomethane offsetting utilities; solar canopy; biodiversity net gain; River Cole green space.
- **Economic** — a £50m catalyst; 4,365 jobs projected across the Tyseley Green Energy Innovation District; research income from year one.
- **Social** — a 20% local construction workforce target; the Community Skills Hub; schools STEM outreach; a green jobs pipeline.
- **Governance** — NEC4 transparency; Tyseley Alliance multi-party oversight; annual public carbon and impact reporting.
