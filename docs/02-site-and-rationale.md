# Site and strategic rationale

## The site

NEXUS sits on a brownfield "green plot" inside Tyseley Energy Park in East Birmingham. The plot is about 8,692 m² with a perimeter near 477.76m and gentle topography (110.46m to 116.71m elevation). Choosing a brownfield plot keeps the environmental impact low and opens the site to land restoration and biodiversity gain rather than consuming greenfield land.

The surrounding park already carries most of the infrastructure the facility needs:

- An existing Veolia waste recovery facility, a source of diverted municipal waste for the anaerobic digestion and BECCS research.
- The Birmingham Energy Innovation Centre.
- Green Biofuels Tyseley, running a hydrogen bus fleet charging facility alongside biodiesel and compressed natural gas fuelling.
- A brownfield plot awarded £20m for the National Centre for the Decarbonisation of Heat.
- A business incubation hub.

A new slip road connects the site to the Small Heath Highway and the city centre, with Tyseley train station nearby and the Grand Union Canal and River Cole alongside for walking and cycling access. Green hydrogen electrolysers, the waste recovery inflow and established logistics already sit on the park, which keeps costs off the £50m budget that a standalone site would carry.

## Why here, and why now

Tyseley is classified as a deprived area, so inward investment and local job creation carry weight with the regional funders the case depends on. The park's existing partnership with the university, its live energy infrastructure, and the neighbouring £20m heat centre completing in 2026 mean NEXUS plugs into an active cluster rather than starting one.

The university's own trajectory supports the timing. Its income grew from £737m in 2019/20 to £1.1bn in 2024/25, with research awards above £280m a year, and its QS World Ranking rose from 84th in 2022/23 to 76th in 2024/25 against a Birmingham 2030 ambition to reach the top 50. That ambition needs flagship research infrastructure of the kind Tyseley does not yet have.

## Financial credibility from precedent

Previous investment at the park shows the location can attract large-scale public funding. The Birmingham Energy Innovation Centre received about £8.5m, and the National Centre for the Decarbonisation of Heat secured £20m of government funding. The £210m Birmingham Health Innovation Campus, delivered through a blended model combining the university, NHS partners, Bruntwood SciTech and Legal & General, is the strongest precedent for the blended funding approach NEXUS proposes. Set against these, a £50m facility reads as an expansion of an existing innovation ecosystem rather than a standalone bet, which lowers the perceived investment risk.

## Community value

The Tyseley plan projects development of 221,500 m² into a Green Energy Innovation District supporting 4,365 jobs across clean energy, materials recycling, industrial symbiosis and advanced manufacturing. A Coalition for Impact programme, launched November 2024, already involves nine community organisations with support from Birmingham City Council's East Birmingham Inclusive Growth Team. NEXUS is designed to connect into this existing engagement and give it a £50m anchor, through a 20% local construction workforce target, the Community Skills Hub, schools outreach and SME affiliate access.
