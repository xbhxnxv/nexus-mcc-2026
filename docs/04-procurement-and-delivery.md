# Procurement and delivery

## Programme

Delivery runs across 42 months in eight phases, from RIBA Stage 1 to practical completion.

| Phase | Year | Milestone |
|-------|------|-----------|
| 0–1 Strategy & surveys | 2026 | Business case confirmed; ground investigation; brownfield site de-risked before capital is committed |
| 2–3 Design & planning | 2026–27 | Concept design developed; planning application submitted; NEC4 target cost agreed |
| 4 Enabling works | 2027 | Brownfield remediation; access improvements; utility diversions |
| 5 Main construction | 2027–28 | Structure, envelope and MEP delivered; BECCS pilot hall frame complete |
| 6 Specialist fit-out | 2028–29 | Labs, digital twin, BMS, V2G, BESS and demonstrator infrastructure installed |
| 7–8 Commissioning & handover | 2029 | Systems tested; soft landing; NEXUS opens |

The phasing is deliberate. Feasibility and surveys come first so the brownfield site is de-risked before any capital is committed, and the target cost is agreed before the main contract, which allows value engineering while there is still room to act on it.

## Procurement route

Two-stage traditional procurement, with design tendered first and construction tendered once the design is developed enough to price. Selective tender runs through the Constructing West Midlands framework, the same route the university used for its £46.5m School of Engineering.

Two routes were considered and set aside. Design and Build was rejected because the specialist lab requirements carry too little design certainty at tender stage for that level of technical complexity. Open tender was rejected because of the Public Procurement Act 2023 administrative burden and the risk of unqualified bidders on a £50m specialist project.

## Contract strategy

| Scope | Contract | Reason |
|-------|----------|--------|
| Main works | NEC4 ECC Option C (target cost, gain/pain share) | Suits first-of-kind BECCS and specialist lab scope; protects the £50m ceiling against cost uncertainty |
| Enabling works | NEC4 ECC Option A (fixed price) | Suits the well-defined remediation, access and utilities scope |
| Professional services | NEC4 Professional Service Contract | Architect, PM, cost consultant, structural and MEP engineers, sustainability advisor |

Tenders are evaluated on technical quality and buildability (25%), cost and value (25%), sustainability and whole-life carbon (20%), experience (10%) and social value (10%).

## The critical near-term action

The single most important next step is to commission an £80,000–£120,000 pre-feasibility study, fundable from the university's Impact Acceleration Accounts, to confirm the technical connection between the NEXUS BECCS laboratory and the existing 10MW biomass plant. That connection is the proposal's defining claim and its highest cost risk. A peer-reviewed technical brief converts NEXUS from a pitch into a bankable asset and unlocks the Tier 1 (UKRI, £20–25m) and Tier 2 (industry, £15–18m) funding conversations before a contractor is appointed.
