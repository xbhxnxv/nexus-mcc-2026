# Facility concept

NEXUS is a single integrated 6,000 m² three-storey research facility. It is organised around three specialist research pillars connected by a shared collaboration spine, so that separate disciplines share infrastructure, data and space rather than sitting in isolation.

## The three research pillars

**BECCS — Bioenergy with Carbon Capture and Storage.** A pilot-scale carbon capture rig connected directly to Tyseley Energy Park's live 10MW biomass plant. No UK university currently operates a pilot-scale BECCS facility; the only comparable installation is Drax power station in Yorkshire, which is a commercial plant rather than an open-access academic testbed. The connection to a working plant is what makes real feedstock characterisation, sorbent research and CO₂ utilisation trials possible on site. Roughly 1,000 m².

**Battery, hydrogen and V2G research.** Next-generation battery degradation laboratories, ATEX-rated green hydrogen electrolysis, second-life EV battery testing, and a vehicle-to-grid testbed linked to the park's hydrogen bus fleet and existing electrolyser infrastructure. The V2G work doubles as a software research stream: bidirectional charging data feeds interface design and feed-in-tariff revenue modelling. Roughly 950 m².

**Sustainable and energy-efficient computing.** An immersion-cooled HPC and AI suite, neuromorphic computing research and carbon-aware algorithm development. This is the university's independent entry into sustainable computing, separate from the Aston-led EPSRC neuromorphic consortium in which Birmingham holds no position. Roughly 450 m².

## The collaboration spine

The pillars connect through a central research street that carries the shared functions:

- A Digital Twin and Building Management System control centre that runs a live model of the building and feeds research data. The building is designed to operate as a research instrument in its own right, with every system (BESS, V2G, solar canopy, BMS) acting as both a live asset and an active testbed.
- Industry partner residency pods and innovation sprint space.
- A Student Transition Studio for live projects and placements.
- A Public Transition Gallery, the "Climate Room", showing live energy data to visitors and schools.
- A Community Skills Hub offering green-skills training to East Birmingham residents.

Outside, a 390 m² Demonstrator Yard holds the V2G charging forecourt, outdoor BESS units, heat pump pilots and active travel infrastructure.

## What NEXUS is not

It does not duplicate the Birmingham Energy Innovation Centre, the National Centre for the Decarbonisation of Heat, or the university's Edgbaston Energy Accelerator Building. Those cover general hydrogen, battery and bioenergy scale-up. NEXUS fills the three gaps none of them address: a BECCS pilot on a live plant, a V2G testbed with real fleet infrastructure, and a sustainable computing lab. It positions itself as the integration and translation layer between assets that already exist.
